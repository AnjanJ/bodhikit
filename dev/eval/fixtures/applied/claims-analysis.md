# Claims in the congestion-charge debate

The case: our city council argued about a 200-rupee daily charge for driving into the old town. The lesson never used it.

1. "Traffic in the old town has risen by a third in five years." Descriptive: it reports something measurable. Traffic counts could show it is false.
2. "Drivers should pay for the road space they take up." Normative: it says what ought to happen. No traffic count can settle it; it needs a principle about who should bear a cost.
3. "The charge will cut traffic by a fifth." Descriptive, even though it is about the future: it is a prediction, and the results from other cities that tried a charge are evidence for or against it.
4. "A charge that falls hardest on poor commuters is unfair." Normative: it judges fairness. But it rests on a descriptive premise, that poorer commuters would pay a bigger share of their income, and that premise can be checked on its own.

Why it matters: the council talked past each other. One side kept answering claim 4 with the traffic data behind claims 1 and 3. The data can test 1 and 3, and the premise inside 4, but never 2 or the verdict in 4. To settle those, they would have to agree on a principle first.
